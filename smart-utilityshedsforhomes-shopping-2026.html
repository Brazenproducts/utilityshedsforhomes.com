Do You Need a Permit for a Shed?

Permit requirements for sheds vary by municipality, but the general rule is: structures under 120 square feet often do not require a building permit in most US jurisdictions. This is why 10x12 and 12x10 shed sizes are so popular — they sit just under the threshold. However, this threshold varies: some jurisdictions allow up to 200 sq ft without a permit, others require permits for any permanent structure regardless of size. Always check your local zoning ordinance before building.

Even when a building permit is not required, other rules may apply. Setback requirements (minimum distance from property lines, fences, and the main structure) apply in almost every jurisdiction. HOA rules may be more restrictive than municipal zoning. Some areas require permits for any structure with a concrete foundation, even small ones. The safest approach is to call your local building department — most will tell you the rules over the phone in 10 minutes.

Wood vs Metal vs Resin Sheds

Wood sheds are the most customizable and have the best resale appeal, but require the most maintenance — painting, staining, and treating for rot every 3–5 years. Metal sheds are low-maintenance and strong but prone to condensation and rust in humid climates. Resin/plastic sheds require zero maintenance and never rust or rot, but look less attractive and provide less insulation than wood or metal.

Foundation Options

A proper foundation extends shed life dramatically. Options from cheapest to most permanent: gravel pad (good drainage, easy DIY), concrete deck blocks (fast, no permit usually required), pressure-treated skids (common for prefab sheds), and poured concrete slab (most permanent, may require permit). For most residential sheds, a gravel pad or concrete deck blocks on level ground is sufficient.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size shed can I build without a permit?

In most US jurisdictions, structures under 120 square feet do not require a building permit. However, this varies — always verify with your local building department. Setback requirements from property lines still apply even for permit-exempt structures.

How long do prefab sheds last?

A well-maintained wood shed lasts 20–30 years. Metal sheds 10–20 years in dry climates, less in humid ones. Resin/plastic sheds 10–20 years depending on UV exposure. Foundation quality is the biggest variable — a shed on a proper gravel or concrete base will outlast one on bare soil by decades.

Can I put a shed on gravel?

Yes — a 4-6 inch compacted gravel pad is one of the best foundations for a shed. It provides excellent drainage, prevents rot under wood floors, and is easy to level. Use crushed gravel (not pea gravel) and compact it thoroughly before placing the shed.
